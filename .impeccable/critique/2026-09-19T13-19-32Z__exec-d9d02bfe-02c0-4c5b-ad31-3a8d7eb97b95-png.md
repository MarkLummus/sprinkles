#### Overall Impression

This is a strong departure from the standard recipe-app gallery: it feels like a serious, personal working notebook. The most valuable next improvement is to make the active item tell a thin, truthful development story rather than presenting only its current version and a reminder.

#### What's Working

- The visual vocabulary is product-specific: identity rails, count marks, paper previews, and maker-authored pen all come from Sprinkles' records rather than a generic dashboard kit.
- The active recipe stays unmistakably primary on desktop, iPad, and phone.
- Ingredients and Kitchen read as shared foundations, while Notebook, Recipe Book, and Idea log remain connected destinations.

#### Priority Issues

- **[P1] Development evidence is too thin in the active recipe.** “2 batches” and “Taste after Sep 20” do not distinguish a batch fact, a tasting status, an observation, and the reason for Version 3. Add one compact labelled chain: `Last batch · Sep 14 · Too hard to scoop` and `Version 3 · less oil`, with the labels carrying the distinctions.
  **Why it matters:** The maker cannot quickly recover what happened or why they should develop, record, or taste next.
  **Fix:** Put this evidence before the actions; retain the maker's exact words where available.
  **Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`.

- **[P1] Sprinkle tallies lack a legible contract.** Their color and quantity feel expressive, but the concept does not say whether they count versions, batches, or activity.
  **Why it matters:** A durable mark system becomes visual noise when the reader must infer its object and count.
  **Fix:** Pair every tally with a textual label and numeral; use a documented tally/rail mapping for the same entity across Home, History, and lists.
  **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`.

- **[P1] Recipe Sheet previews are generic lined-paper glyphs.** They suggest paper but do not yet prove that the object is the Recipe Sheet.
  **Why it matters:** The preview loses its product-specific job and competes with more useful batch evidence, particularly on phone.
  **Fix:** Give every thumbnail a tiny repeatable anatomy: title, formula rows, method block, and a small batch-log mark; label it `Recipe Sheet` where the context does not already establish it.
  **Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`.

- **[P2] Ask an expert is contextually placed but generic in wording.**
  **Why it matters:** Its placement avoids a floating-chat anti-pattern, but the label does not state what evidence will be used.
  **Fix:** Use `Ask about Olive Oil Ice Cream` or a concise supporting line such as `Use this version and its batches.`
  **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`.

- **[P2] The small-screen hierarchy overweights the Sheet preview.**
  **Why it matters:** The note thumbnail gets attention before the batch result or next action that would let a maker resume work.
  **Fix:** Reduce the thumbnail on phone and place the last observation or tasting state directly above the actions.
  **Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`.

#### Persona Red Flags

- **Returning maker:** After kitchen work, the maker needs the last result in their own words. The concept shows a future reminder but no recoverable batch observation.
- **Serious experimenter:** They need lineage without causal overclaim. The concept does not yet show that “Less oil” responds to a particular record.
- **Maker with a growing collection:** Color is appropriately limited to identity, but tallies need explicit rules before the library expands.

#### Minor Observations

- The desktop side note adds atmosphere but does not help the next task.
- iPad navigation is near its density limit if another foundation or utility is added.
- The action order is good, but the evidence that would help choose among them needs to come first.

#### Questions to Consider

- Can the hero show `Version → last batch → observation → next change` in one compact line without turning into an analytics panel?
- If the Recipe Sheet preview disappeared, would the maker still know what happened and what to do next?
- Should “Make next” mix recipes, batches, and ideas, or should the row grammar visibly distinguish their different kinds of work?
