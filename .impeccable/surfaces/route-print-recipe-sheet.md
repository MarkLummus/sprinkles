## 1. Job and audience

Mark, at a desktop, with a saved version he intends to make. He prints, carries paper to the kitchen, and works from it at the machine — hands wet, scale beside him, app not present. Days pass. He returns to the app with the sheet, or a photo of it, and types in what happened. He hole-punches the printout and keeps it in a binder, on either side of a duplex sheet.

The sheet's job is to be **the batch's first record** — not a reference copy of the screen, but the artifact that carries ink, whose ink is later transcribed. `PRODUCT.md`'s operating context is explicit: desktop formulates, paper works the kitchen, phone transcribes. The binder confirms the ritual: weigh with an As made line per row, write over printed grams when reality differs, note process values sparsely, record a verdict about half the time (15 of 29 sheets), carry a "to fix" list forward by hand.

Success: he makes the batch without reaching for the app, and returns with a sheet whose every mark has a home in the app's record.

## 2. Outcome and proof

Primary task: print a saved version as a bench sheet, make from it, come back with it filled in. Secondary: reach a version from the code on a sheet in hand.

Success is PRINT-01…05 against real content — the olive oil working case: twelve rows (some split into portions), ten steps with typed targets, three carried-forward notes, two before-you-start notes, 800 g, two 16 oz jars, circulator bath, Whynter machine, 1 g kitchen scale plus 0.01 g precision scale.

The product-specific truth a generic recipe printout could not claim: **the sheet knows which version it is.** A short code ties the paper back to an exact formulation, so a record made in October attaches to the version it was actually made from, not to whatever that recipe has become since. This is the print-side expression of the product's central separation (D04) and of its promise that editing today never rewrites yesterday.

## 3. Selected direction

**Visual authority: the Cupping Form, unchanged.** This surface needs no new world and no world workshop — the world was designed for exactly this. Printed black, recorded ink blue, the two never mixing; colour identifies and form carries state; light only, forced by a kitchen table in the evening and a black-only laser printer; print-native throughout. For three phases the screen has been imitating the sheet. Here the sheet stops being a metaphor and becomes the thing itself, and the existing tokens, faces, rules, ticks and scales carry over without invention.

**Structural thesis: the blue layer prints as blank space.** Everything the system knows prints in black. Everywhere the maker will write, the sheet prints nothing but a rule, a box, or a scale — the exact geometry the app will later ask him to fill. Paper and screen are one form in two materials, and a real pen does on paper what pen blue does on screen. Nothing on the sheet is a second design of anything already designed.

### Rules for every printed page (sketch 010 D7, D16, D20, D22; `CONTRACT.md` § 1)

- **Page.** Letter portrait only, white, ink only (`--sheet-ink`; one colour measured on the log pages and the Instructions). Smallest type 12 px. No controls, no hover, no focus: paper. The app sets no `print-color-adjust`, so both engines drop the Sheet's ground and the page is white.
- **Margins** (D16, then D20; Mark, `decide-hole-punch-duplex` both sides, `decide-hole-punch-margin-width` 0.75 in): 0.75 in at the left and the right, 0.5 in above and below, on every page. He hole-punches the sheet, and a duplex back page is punched on its right. `@page { size: letter; margin: 0.5in 0.75in }`. The content box is 672 × 960 in Chrome and 630 × 900 in Safari (WebKit prints 1 CSS px as 0.8 pt). The print layout must not read the window's width: the live app's phone rules start below 724 px, and the content box is under that.
- **The 2 px guard** (D22): on the log pages the ink stops 2 px short of the content box's right edge, because WebKit's print clips the date box's right border otherwise.
- **Foot in flow** (D7): Safari cannot print a running foot or "Page n of m", so there is none of either. Each log page carries its foot inside the page, after its content with at least 16 px of white above the rule, and the Sheet carries one after its last step. No page n of m on the formula and Instructions pages.
- **Order on paper** (Phase 4 D-07a): the Batch log (side 1) and the Tasting log (side 2), one sheet front and back, then the Sheet's pages. The log is in front, ahead of the formula page.
- **Sheet code** in every foot: derived from the version (§ 7). The word "Sheet code" is on no foot (Mark, `decide-foot-sheet-code-word`: leave the bare code); the screen route's line carries the word.

**Core and declared items** (D25, D26; `CONTRACT.md` § 2). The log pages, the pen and the reading mix items every recipe has with items only some recipes declare. **Core** items print for every recipe: page 1's title, dates, figures, At draw and Notes; page 2's figures, Melt style, From freezer, the four core scales, the four core problems and Notes; both feet. Page 2 keeps 5 writing lines whatever the count, because the problems row holds up to six boxes on one line at both widths. Tests are parameterised by recipe and take the expected declared list from each version, never from the Olive Oil boards. Defining declared fields for a net-new recipe is a later task.

**Focal moment: the as-made column.** The As made line opens every row, and the printed gram sits in black beside it: empty space the width of a written number, first in the row. The sheet asks to be corrected, and that invitation is the product's whole thesis on paper — the plan is not the record, and what was actually done is worth its own column.

**Implementation consequence.** A route of its own, `/notebook/:recipeId/:versionId/print` (the Assets and processes amendment), rendering from the saved version record. `router.jsx` already anticipates it ("Phase 2's batch route and Phase 4's print route are additions, not a retrofit"). ## 4. Scope and boundaries

**Fidelity:** production-ready. **Breadth:** the print route and the printed set (the two log sides and the Sheet's three pages for a long table, or two for a short one). **Interactivity:** print, and enter a code to reach a version.

**Touched elsewhere, minimally** (all drawn in sketch 010 § 8): one **Print sheet** control on the version row (closing `route-recipe.md` § 7's open item "Where Print sits on the version row"); on Home, an **Enter Sheet code** link at the right of the Recipes row to the Search page, where the code field lives (Phase 4 D-10, D-13; the field and "No version has this code." are not drawn); on Home, under the lead's version line, **"Draft open on this version · Go to draft"**, with the recipe that has the open draft taking the lead (Mark, `decide-p4-home-draft-on-other-recipe`), the list below keeping its order (`decide-home-list-order-with-draft-lead`); and the draft-kept notice over the reopened Next version pen, **"Draft kept. Cancel discards it."**, clearing after 5 s and not persisted (Phase 4 D-15; Mark, `decide-p4-draft-kept-words` C, `decide-p4-draft-kept-lifetime`).

**Untouched:** the recipe page's reading and pen states beyond what sketch 010 § 7 draws (the record pen and its reading take At draw and From freezer and lose Time to draw temp.; the record brief owns them), the batch record's own flow, the front-matter rows, the store's schema beyond what a derived code needs (which is nothing). Whether the app drops the stored `timeToDrawTempMinutes` field is a build decision (Mark's List row `build-remove-draw-temp-data-element`); the printed page already omits it.

**Out, and named:** QR codes and the print-log "did you churn it?" nudge, both retired and not revived; a mode picker or toggle; publishing and sharing output, and the one-page Recipe book format (`RecipeBookSheet`, Mark's List row `todo-recipe-book-print-format`, phase 04+); printing a batch record or a filled log; printing the show-changes state; scaling; UX1-02 draft persistence across a reload (in GSD Phase 4, but not this surface).

**Anti-goals:** no colour anywhere on the sheet, section headings included (they print in ink, not bookcloth green; sketch 010 E5); a white page, which is also how both browsers print the Sheet today; no pass/fail or score mark on a printed figure; no freezing curve, derived advisories (Watch for) or anything else from column two except Balance, which prints beside the ingredient table as the version's assessment (Mark, 2026-10-06, sketch 010 E4); no screen controls on paper, Balance's Hide included; no strikes, removed rows, removed steps, or parent values — the sheet prints the clean reading (`route-recipe-version.md` § 6); no batch record created by printing (PRINT-05); no browser header or footer standing in for the sheet's own foot; no "Page n of m" anywhere (D7); no "Time to draw temp." on paper (D17); no "Every recipe" or "This recipe only" cue on paper (D23); no text smaller than 12 px; no second design of a control that already exists on screen.

## 5. States and ranges

Twelve rows typical, one to about twenty-four plausible; a row carries one to three portions; ten steps typical, three to twenty plausible; two declared scales, zero to two; four core problems plus zero to two declared; targets on some steps and none on others; an Ingredients table that fits its page or breaks (Olive Oil v1's does).

**Material states:** a version never churned · a version already churned (the log still prints blank, because it is for the next batch) · a one-portion row · a row split across two steps · a step with no targets · a recipe with no declared scales and no declared problems (page 2 carries only the core rows; Mexican Chocolate v4, 729 px used of Safari's 900) · a recipe with one or two of each · a table that breaks across pages with Balance breaking between gauges · a table that fits, so the Instructions open their own page · a method long enough to take three pages · the route reached for a version with unsaved edits elsewhere (it prints the saved record and says so) · a code that matches no version · a code entered with or without its hyphen.

## 6. Interaction and layout

- **On screen.** The route states recipe, version line, saved date, and Sheet code in one line, then renders the pages as they will print, then offers a Print control (§ 3, "The route on screen"). Keyboard-operable with visible labels, visible focus and AA text contrast, like every other surface (UX1-01, UX1-03).
- **On paper.** Every log page carries its foot: recipe · version line · details · Sheet code · "Side n of 2". Duplex sheets get separated in a binder, so the code is on both sides of the log, not the front alone. The Sheet's pages carry no foot until the closing foot after step 10.
- **The mise-en-place box is retired** (Mark, 2026-10-06, sketch 010 E1): the As made line does its job. The ingredient table prints no tick box.
- **A blank scale** is one rule with five ticks and none filled, anchors in italic at its ends and "right" under the middle tick. Marking side 2 by hand and marking the app on return are then the same gesture on the same form.
- **A blank line** is a hairline ink baseline and nothing else — no label, no border — exactly as `route-recipe.md` § 3 already rules blank prose fields on screen. A writing line is 32 px; a figure line is 96 px.
- **A mark-one row** is the option words, each preceded by its own 14 px tick box, since paper has no filled state. At draw and From freezer are the two groups that head the evaluation fields on paper; in the app they are a two-option control (Nothing to note, Not evaluated) that hides the group's fields when picked (sketch 010 § 7, the record brief's).
- **Rhythm** (D22): section heads 14 px, 600, capitals, with the cue at the right at 12 px; 32 px of space above every section; the sub-group cue in the app's group-cue face, 14 px, 600, sentence case; a title 32 px, 12 px under it. The Sheet's own rhythm (20 px between regions, 6 px under each head) and the Instructions' (steps 20 px apart; 6, 6 and 12 px inside a step) are held.
- No motion, no hover, no interactive state in the sheet's own rendering; the world has no motion at all.

## 7. Constraints and open decisions

**Binding.** One component tree sharing the recipe page's primitives wherever one exists; every visual value read through a custom property in `app/src/styles/tokens.css`, with `@media print` blocks as top-level siblings and never nested; labels from `product-requirements/05-domain-and-language.md`, which retires the old prototype's *Come-up*, *Draw*, *Overrun* and *Meltdown* wholesale; notes and prose rendered as text, never markup. The pages are sized to the content box, not to the window, and the foot stays in flow. `tabIndex={0}` on every link, button, radio and checkbox the route renders (`tabindex-scan.test.js`).

**The short code.** Derived from the version id — the same version always prints the same code, reprints match, nothing is stored, and matching back is a lookup. Crockford base32 grouped `XXXX-XXXX` (from the prototype's `sheet-codes.js`) is a sound starting point **for the alphabet and the grouping only**; its per-print random nonce is deliberately not carried over. Accepted loss, stated plainly: two printings of one unchanged version are indistinguishable, and the app cannot tell Tuesday's sheet from Friday's. Accepted because the batch record is created when the batch is *recorded*, never when it is printed — settled product research, not an open question, and PRINT-05 already says so. A derived code also leaves D16 (storage) untouched, which an open decision deserves. `[short code]` on the log boards and K7QM-2X9D on the screens are examples, not derived codes.

**Modes, recorded and not built.** Printing will later carry more than one mode: the bench sheet for recipe development, and a publishing or sharing output. Milestone 1 builds the bench sheet alone. This is the reason printing takes its own route rather than print CSS on the recipe page — the recipe page is already a complex surface and must not absorb a second job. The route must leave room for a mode without inventing one: no mode switcher, no toggle, and no abstraction built to serve a single case.

**A builder must not invent:** the code's alphabet, length or grouping; which fields appear on which side of the batch log; page-break positions within the batch log; any printed colour; any field on paper that the app has no home for on return; a declared scale or problem a version does not list.
