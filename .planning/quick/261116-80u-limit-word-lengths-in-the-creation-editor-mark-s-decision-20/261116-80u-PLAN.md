<objective>
Build Mark's decision of 2026-11-16 (12-UI-REVIEW.md finding 2, option b, Sid's recommendation): limit the words a maker types for a recipe's own scales and problems in the creation editor. A scale's name may hold at most 16 characters, each of its end words at most 12, and a problem at most 40, all counted on the trimmed text. Words over a limit are refused at Save in the existing Save-refusal pattern: a sentence under the field, nothing written, focus on the first refused field. The rule lives in app/src/domain/creation.js with tests. No stored shape changes and import validation is unchanged.

Purpose: one rule mends the record pen's run-together end words (finding 1), the printed Tasting log's crowding and its spill onto a third sheet in Safari (finding 2 and 12-REVIEW.md WR-01), because the measured worst case can no longer be typed. The Phase 12 harness's long-words case then holds the longest allowed words and checks them (PASS or FAIL) instead of reporting them, and sketch 015 draws the refused state so the boards stay the single authority.

Output: the domain rule, the gate and their tests (Task 1, the tracer); the harness's long-words case reset and turned into checks (Task 2); a new sketch 015 board of the refused state with its board spec (Task 3).

Decisions taken in this plan (Claude's discretion, inside Mark's decision):
- The sentence is `Use N characters or fewer.` (N is 16, 12 or 40). It is Claude's wording in the shape of the gate's existing sentences ("Add at least one ingredient.", "This problem is already listed."), for Mark in chat, recorded as such in the code comment and the SUMMARY.
- A refused field reuses its existing block id (`scale:<key>:<field>`, `problem:<index>`), because a field is either blank (Required) or too long, never both. CreationSetup.jsx already reads those ids, so no component changes. A problem that is too long is refused for its length alone; two identical problems are either both too long or both within the limit, so the repeat check loses nothing.
- Characters are counted as code points of the trimmed text (an emoji counts once), so the sentence's "characters" matches what the maker sees in ordinary text.
- The limits bound realistic words, not glyph widths: twelve capital W measure 150 px at the pen's anchors face. The SUMMARY records this as a residual; nothing is built for it.
- The refused panel on the new board uses a Right in the middle scale, so 261116-80v (no numbers on a Low to high scale) does not touch it.

What planning measured (WebKit, the pen's anchors face: 13 px italic system grotesk; the print's end-word face: 13 px italic Georgia; the print name: 12 px weight 500 caps, 0.04em tracking): pen "barely a few" 73.2, "right" 28.3, "far too many" 76.0, "barely there" 72.7, "overwhelming" 84.5 px; print name "CHOCOLATE FLECKS" 130.7 and "BURNT SUGAR NOTE" 130.8 of the 150 px column; print low words 72.6 and 71.0 of the 76 px column. So a Right in the middle scale with two 12-character ends fills about 177.5 of the 186 px track at 768, about 4.25 px a gap. That is under the 6 px that finding 1's fix names. It is a matter for Mark (accept, a lower end limit for that kind, or a wider track). This plan builds the decided limits, reports the gap, and does not claim finding 1 closed for that one case.
